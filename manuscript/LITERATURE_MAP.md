# Literature map for the manuscript

This file maps candidate references to specific manuscript claims. It is intentionally conservative: a reference is listed only for claims that its reported data or abstract directly support. Patents are separated from peer-reviewed literature and should be cited as formulation/practice evidence rather than as independent mechanistic proof.

## A. Reactive-PUR rheology and prepolymer state

### 1. Paruta-Tuarez, Marchal & Choplin (2014)
**Title:** Application of the systemic rheology to the in situ follow-up of viscosity evolution with reaction time in the synthesis of urethane prepolymers  
**Journal:** International Journal of Adhesion and Adhesives 50, 32-36  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.ijadhadh.2013.12.015

**Use in this manuscript:**
- supports the statement that viscosity evolves during urethane-prepolymer synthesis and therefore depends on reaction history, not composition and temperature alone;
- supports the use of rheology as a process-sensitive observable;
- directly relevant to PPG/MDI-type prepolymer chemistry.

**Do not use it to claim:** that our latent realization offset is caused by one specific reaction pathway.

### 2. Sebenik & Krajnc (2007)
**Title:** Influence of the soft segment length and content on the synthesis and properties of isocyanate-terminated urethane prepolymers  
**Journal:** International Journal of Adhesion and Adhesives 27(7), 527-535  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.ijadhadh.2006.10.001

**Use in this manuscript:**
- supports composition dependence of prepolymer molecular weight and Brookfield viscosity;
- supports effects of NCO/OH ratio and polyol molecular weight/soft-segment composition;
- useful background for the E1-E5 design axes.

### 3. Heintz et al. (2003)
**Title:** Effects of Reaction Temperature on the Formation of Polyurethane Prepolymer Structures  
**Journal:** Macromolecules 36(8), 2695-2704  
**DOI:** 10.1021/ma021559h

**Use in this manuscript:**
- supports the statement that reaction temperature changes prepolymer structure and side-reaction extent;
- supports treating thermal history as chemically relevant in reactive polyurethane systems.

**Boundary:** the reported high-temperature allophanate chemistry should not be assumed to dominate our 80-130 C measurements without direct chemical evidence.

### 4. Duffy et al. (2005)
**Title:** The competitive influence of specific interactions and extent of reaction on the miscibility of ternary reactive polymer blends: model for polyurethane adhesives  
**Journal:** International Journal of Adhesion and Adhesives 25(1), 39-46  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.ijadhadh.2004.03.003

**Use in this manuscript:**
- supports the broader idea that the realized state of reactive PU-containing blends changes during prepolymer formation;
- especially relevant because the model blend included polyether, polyester and acrylic components.

## B. Temperature-dependent viscosity modeling

### 5. Pugar et al. (2025)
**Title:** Machine learning of polyurethane prepolymer viscosity: a comparison of chemical and physicochemical approaches  
**Journal:** Digital Discovery 4, 3652-3661  
**DOI:** 10.1039/D5DD00287G

**Use in this manuscript:**
- directly supports temperature-dependent viscosity as a central processing variable for PUR prepolymers;
- provides a contemporary comparison of composition-based and physically informed viscosity models;
- provides the external 39-prepolymer viscosity library underlying part of our curated database;
- supports use of an Andrade-like representation for continuous viscosity-temperature behavior.

**Contrast with our work:** their primary modeling target is chemistry-to-viscosity/thermal descriptor prediction; our local contribution is realization-conditioned scale shifts and one-anchor calibration within a narrow chemistry family.

## C. Reactive hot-melt adhesive structure, curing and processing

### 6. Cui et al. (2002)
**Title:** Crystalline structure in isocyanate reactive hot melt adhesives  
**Journal:** International Journal of Adhesion and Adhesives 22(4), 317-322  
**DOI:** 10.1016/S0143-7496(02)00010-6

**Use in this manuscript:**
- supports the distinction between melt/process behavior, green strength and later moisture curing;
- supports the importance of physical structure/crystallization in reactive hot-melt adhesive performance.

### 7. Cui et al. (2003)
**Title:** Evaluation of the cure kinetics of isocyanate reactive hot-melt adhesives with differential scanning calorimetry  
**Journal:** Journal of Applied Polymer Science 89(10), 2708-2713  
**DOI:** 10.1002/app.12394

**Use in this manuscript:**
- supports that reactive hot-melt adhesives continue to undergo chemically driven evolution after application;
- useful for distinguishing our rheological temperature-sensitivity descriptor from a true cure activation energy.

### 8. Jeong et al. (2009)
**Title:** The Properties of Reactive Hot Melt Polyurethane Adhesives: Effects of Molecular Weight and Reactive Organoclay  
**Journal:** Polymer-Plastics Technology and Engineering 48(9), 932-938  
**DOI:** 10.1080/03602550902995034

**Use in this manuscript:**
- shows that the preparation route can change molecular weight and viscosity even at the same nominal recipe;
- supports the general proposition that processing route and modifier state affect both rheology and adhesion.

### 9. Jeong et al. (2007)
**Title:** The effect of organoclay on the properties of a reactive hot melt polyurethane adhesive  
**Journal:** Composite Interfaces 14(5-6), 467-476  
**DOI:** 10.1163/156855407781291227

**Use in this manuscript:**
- supports rheology as an independently modified property in reactive PUR hot melts;
- demonstrates that a formulation additive can simultaneously alter viscosity, yield-like behavior, set time and bond strength.

## D. Acrylic/resin modification as a defensible formulation direction

### 10. Jung et al. (2008)
**Title:** Properties of reactive hot melt polyurethane adhesives with acrylic polymer or macromonomer modifications  
**Journal:** Journal of Applied Polymer Science 109(3), 1757-1763  
**DOI:** 10.1002/app.28291

**Use in this manuscript:**
- strong peer-reviewed support for acrylic modification of reactive PU hot-melt adhesives;
- reports increased melt viscosity and initial bond strength when part of the polyol/tackifier fraction is replaced by acrylic polymer or reactive acrylic macromonomer;
- directly supports using acrylic chemistry as a formulation axis, without implying an exact optimum for our AC1920/TK100 system.

### 11. Kim et al. (2008)
**Title:** Acrylic copolymer intercalated in sodium montmorillonite: a modifier of reactive hot melt polyurethane adhesive  
**Journal:** Composite Interfaces 15(6), 577-587  
**DOI:** 10.1163/156855408785971290

**Use in this manuscript:**
- supports acrylic-containing modifiers as a route to alter initial bond strength, set time and rheology.

### 12. Cho, Jeong & Kim (2009)
**Title:** Reactive Hot Melt Polyurethane Adhesives Modified by Acrylic Copolymer Nanocomposites  
**Journal:** Macromolecular Research 17(11), 879-885  
**DOI:** 10.1007/BF03218630

**Use in this manuscript:**
- additional independent support that acrylic-containing modifiers alter melt viscosity and early bond-strength development.

### 13. Ruan, Peng & Wang (2021)
**Title:** Preparation and properties of polyacrylate resin/reactive polyurethane hot melt adhesive  
**Journal:** China Adhesives 30(1), 12-17  
**DOI:** 10.13416/j.ca.2021.01.004

**Use in this manuscript:**
- directly relevant formulation evidence for incorporating polyacrylate resin into PURHMA;
- reports changes in morphology, open time and bonding performance with acrylic-resin addition.

### 14. Xiao, Ouyang & Li (2025)
**Title:** Preparation and performance study of high temperature resistant reactive polyurethane hot melt adhesive for electronic products  
**Journal:** China Adhesives 34(9), 54-59  
**DOI:** 10.13416/j.ca.2025.09.008

**Use in this manuscript:**
- contemporary evidence that acrylic-resin content is a practically tunable variable in high-temperature PUR formulation;
- the paper reports 15% acrylic resin as the preferred level within its own formulation system.

**Boundary:** use the 15% value only as a literature-specific formulation point, not as a universal optimum and not as proof of our validation recipe.

## E. Broader formulation-space evidence

### 15. Ruan et al. (2019)
**Title:** Bio-polyols synthesized from bio-based 1,3-propanediol and applications on polyurethane reactive hot melt adhesives  
**Journal:** Industrial Crops and Products 128, 436-444  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.indcrop.2018.11.045

**Use in this manuscript:**
- demonstrates that polyol chemistry and compatibility substantially alter open time, green strength and adhesion;
- supports the broad chemistry-landscape role of the external database.

### 16. Liu et al. (2020)
**Title:** CO2-based poly(propylene carbonate) with various carbonate linkage content for reactive hot-melt polyurethane adhesives  
**Journal:** International Journal of Adhesion and Adhesives 96, 102456  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.ijadhadh.2019.102456

**Use in this manuscript:**
- supports strong dependence of reactive hot-melt performance on polyol chemistry and carbonate linkage content;
- useful evidence that local thermal/rheological regularities should not be generalized across all PUR chemistry families.

### 17. Sun et al. (2022)
**Title:** Reactive polyurethane hot melt adhesives based on polycarbonate and sebacic acid-based polyester polyols  
**Journal:** Journal of Adhesion Science and Technology 37(4), 663-682  
**DOI:** 10.1080/01694243.2022.2036407

**Use in this manuscript:**
- further supports formulation-dependent HMPUR behavior across polyol molecular weight and chemistry.

## G. Supporting patent/practice evidence (not substitutes for peer-reviewed mechanism)

### US5932680A - Moisture-curing polyurethane hot-melt adhesive
Use for: historical/practical precedent that melt stability is operationally evaluated by viscosity increase during prolonged elevated-temperature holding; also supports resin/tackifier formulation ranges.

### US6465104B1 - Modified polyurethane hotmelt adhesive
Use for: unusually direct evidence that acrylic-copolymer hydroxyl functionality can change prolonged hot-melt viscosity stability at 121 C. Example 10 increased 37% over 0.5-8 h, compared with 76% for a higher-OH acrylic comparator.

## Manuscript citation plan

- **Introduction paragraph 1 (reactive PUR processing/rheology):** Sebenik & Krajnc 2007; Paruta-Tuarez et al. 2014; Pugar et al. 2025.
- **Introduction paragraph 2 (history/state sensitivity):** Heintz et al. 2003; Paruta-Tuarez et al. 2014; Jeong et al. 2009.
- **Introduction paragraph 3 (static viscosity vs evolving/cure response):** Cui et al. 2002; Cui et al. 2003.
- **Results 2.2/2.3 (temperature modeling):** Pugar et al. 2025 as the closest contemporary comparison; frame our state-shift and anchor-calibration result as complementary rather than claiming prior art absence.
- **Results 2.4 (distinct temporal-stability coordinate):** Paruta-Tuarez et al. 2014 plus the melt-stability patent/practice sources; our quantitative E1/E5/F1 separation remains the local evidence.
- **Results 2.5 (resin/acrylic intervention):** Jung et al. 2008; Kim et al. 2008; Cho et al. 2009; Ruan et al. 2021; Xiao et al. 2025.
- **Agent section:** Boiko et al. 2023; Bran et al. 2024.

## References to avoid overusing

- Do not cite generic polyurethane reviews for claims that can be supported by the primary papers above.
- Do not treat patents as proof of molecular mechanism.
- Do not cite the 39-curve Pugar dataset as proof that the local ~42 kJ/mol scale is universal; their broader chemistry space is useful precisely because it shows wider variation.
- Do not use acrylic-modification papers to claim that AC1920 or TK100 individually caused the observed validation outcome; the current validation formulation changes multiple components simultaneously.

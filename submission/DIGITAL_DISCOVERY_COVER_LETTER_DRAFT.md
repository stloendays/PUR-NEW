Dear Editors of *Digital Discovery*,

Please consider our manuscript, **"Rheological State Identification Guides Hypothesis-Driven Experiment Selection in Reactive Polyurethane Hot-Melt Adhesives"**, for publication as a Full Paper.

The study addresses a practical obstacle in reactive materials discovery: a recipe may be known precisely while the measured material state and the processing failure mode remain uncertain. For reactive polyurethane hot-melt adhesives, we find that nominally identical formulations can occupy substantially different viscosity states while retaining a common local temperature response. More importantly, this temperature-dependent response can be distinguished from the formulation-sensitive viscosity increase that occurs during thermal residence. We treat that separation as a physical basis for deciding which measurement will be useful next.

Three aspects distinguish this work from a conventional formulation-to-property prediction study. First, we show that one actual viscosity reading can locate a new rheological state and independently validate its chemistry-dependent transfer boundary against published original prepolymer measurements. Second, we establish the physical relevance of a thermal-hold stability objective through direct measurements and a quantitative dilution-based null model. Third, we use those findings to define explicit, chemically admissible formulation–measurement experiments and test how rule content and ordering affect the decisions of language-model-based scientific workflows. The determinant of useful selection is an auditable scientific decision policy, rather than an unconstrained claim of model autonomy.

This integration of experimental materials science, interpretable quantitative modelling and reproducible experiment selection is directly relevant to *Digital Discovery*: the paper demonstrates how prior scientific knowledge can be turned into a testable next experiment, not simply a better retrospective predictor. The manuscript is accompanied by public source measurements, analysis scripts, figures, frozen computational decision records and supplementary reproducibility material. Newly planned physical measurements are explicitly distinguished from the completed validation.

Thank you for considering the work.

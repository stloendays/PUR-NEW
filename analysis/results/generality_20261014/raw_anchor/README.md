# Raw-observation conductivity transfer: independent validation

## Scientific question

Can the transferable temperature-response geometry, identified from reactive PUR
and compared against solid polymer-electrolyte (SPE) ionic-conductivity curves,
support a true **one-measured-reading** transfer test, rather than reconstruction
of fitted values that already used the entire held curve?

This is a *cross-material property-curve analogy*, not evidence of a shared
chemical or molecular transport mechanism. Conductivity is not viscosity.

## Prediction contract

For each eligible host and held formulation curve:

1. Fit the common quadratic response `ln(sigma)=a_curve+b1*z(T)+b2*z(T)^2`
   with a **distinct intercept for each *training* curve**. Curves in the
   training pool may be represented by their own smoothed temperature grids.
2. For the held-out curve, obtain **only one actual source measurement**:
   its highest observed temperature within the shared host window. That
   measured log-conductivity fixes its level; no value of the held curve is
   used in fitting `b1` or `b2`.
3. Predict the held curve's other *raw*, strictly colder source observations.
   Never compare fitted held-curve grid values to other fitted grid values.
4. Compute `exp(sqrt(mean(error_ln_sigma^2)))` over the exact held targets;
   also compare against the host-level mean response without the reading.
5. Repeat at two levels of independence:
   - **Curve holdout**: exclude the full target curve from fitting.
   - **Publication holdout**: additionally exclude all *other* training curves
     whose source DOI matches the held curve; this scope is available only
     where the same polymer backbone is represented in multiple studies.

A target's measured-temperature range is used only to establish in-domain
eligibility and the location of its warmest available reading. Temperature
availability is therefore conditioned on the source curve, while the held-out
conductivity targets are never used to train shape or calibrate intercept.

## Results independently executed by GitHub Actions

From `summary.json`, generated by the `--check` run on 14 October 2026:

| Validation | ≤10 °C multiplicative RMSE | ≤20 °C multiplicative RMSE | ≤30 °C multiplicative RMSE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Curve-held-out, actual measured points | 1.305× (470 predictions) | 1.659× (979 predictions; 322 curves, 14 hosts) | 2.251× (1,476 predictions) |
| Publication-held-out, actual measured points | 1.316× (368 predictions) | 1.705× (767 predictions; 232 curves, 5 hosts, 53 DOIs) | 2.426× (1,163 predictions) |

**At ≤20 °C**, 91.9% of the curve-held-out raw predictions are within
twofold, compared with a **10.949×** host-average no-anchor baseline on exactly
those predictions. A publication-held-out model is within twofold for
92.3% of its target observations (baseline 13.187×).

Sensitivity to family imbalance, curve-held-out raw observations at ≤20 °C:

- The largest host (`[Cu]OCC[Au]`) contributes 163 source curves. After
  removing that host: **1.495×** across 414 targets, 159 curves and 13 hosts.
- With an equal **family-level mean squared log-error** weighting:
  **1.614×** across all 14 hosts (not the prediction-weighted pooled 1.659×).

The independent stricter test confirms that the gain is not an artifact of
learning a held-out conductivity response from its own smoother. It also
shows a **clear deterioration with temperature distance**. The transferable
object is a *host- and temperature-window-bounded empirical response*.

## Why the 2026-10-08 1.68× result remains separate

The earlier `1.68×` result uses *interpolated* values: before the
curve-level holdout, every curve is smoothed from **all its measurements**
and placed on a 5 °C temperature grid. Its stated anchor is the warmest
*grid value*, not a single held-out laboratory reading. Thus it is useful
for comparing smooth-curve geometry but cannot alone validate a
real-world one-reading procedure.

The new held-out target is an *original source conductivity observation*
and its anchor is an independent *different original source observation*.
**The 1.68×/1.659× statistics use different evaluation targets and are not
paired model improvements.** Do not subtract them as if they shared a test set.

## Mechanistic and experimental boundary

- Host identity is *known*; this is **not** prediction for an unseen polymer
  family, nor a forecast of reactive-PUR hold-time viscosity drift.
- The conductivity data are aggregated from literature, not newly measured
  in this project. Differences between papers in salt, molecular weight,
  conductivity techniques and sample preparation remain a source of variance.
- 9 of 14 hosts do not have multiple source papers in the eligible curve set;
  they correctly remain outside the publication-held-out validation.
- For the PUR chemistry, the next decisive wet-lab evidence remains the
  independently registered **S1C39 acrylic-only** versus S1C41 dual-axis
  matched thermal-hold contrast, followed by the two **F1 80–130 °C** sweeps.
  Those are planned, not measured. They can determine whether resin
  modification changes the local rheological shape and test H-RESIN/H-DUAL
  at the formulation level; the SPE analogy cannot answer either question.
